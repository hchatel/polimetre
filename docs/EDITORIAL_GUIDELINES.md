# Editorial Guidelines

## 1. Objective

Polimetre should help users understand political facts without telling them what to think.

The editorial standard is:

> Separate facts, positions, interpretations and opinions.

---

## 2. Facts

Facts should be stated directly and sourced.

Example:

> "The National Assembly adopted the bill on 14 March."

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

---

## 3. Political Positions

Attribute positions explicitly.

Prefer:

> "The X group voted against the bill."

Avoid:

> "X rejected this important reform."

The latter adds an interpretation.

---

## 4. Arguments

When presenting arguments for and against a measure, attribute them.

Prefer:

> "Supporters of the measure argued that..."

> "Opponents argued that..."

Do not present political arguments as objective facts.

Attribute arguments to supporters or opponents of the measure, **not to a party or group**. Users should judge an argument on its merits before seeing who voted what.

Present arguments for and against with comparable length and care.

---

## 5. Neutral Language

Avoid loaded adjectives such as:

* radical;
* extreme;
* irresponsible;
* courageous;
* absurd;
* dangerous;
* historic;

unless they are part of a directly attributed quotation or clearly relevant factual description.

---

## 6. Titles

Titles should describe the issue rather than frame the conclusion.

Bad:

> "The government finally fixes pensions."

Better:

> "The pension reform changes the retirement age."

---

## 7. Questions

Quiz questions must not contain hidden arguments.

Bad:

> "Should France stop its disastrous tax policies?"

Better:

> "Should income taxes be reduced?"

---

## 8. Political Entities

Use the official or commonly accepted name of political groups.

Do not use nicknames or derogatory labels.

---

## 9. Uncertainty

When evidence is incomplete, say so.

Useful formulations:

> "The available voting record shows..."

> "No parliamentary vote by the candidate is available for this issue."

> "This comparison is based on the group's voting record, not a direct vote by the candidate."

Uncertainty is preferable to false precision.

---

## 10. AI-Assisted Content

AI-generated content must be considered a draft.

Before publication, a human reviewer should verify:

* factual claims;
* sources;
* wording;
* political attribution;
* omissions;
* misleading simplifications.

The reviewer should be able to reject or edit the generated content.
